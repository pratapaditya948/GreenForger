What is Hydroponics?

Hydroponics is a way of growing plants without soil. Instead, the roots sit in water that carries all the nutrients the plant needs, or in a light material such as coco peat or clay pebbles that is fed with that water.

Because the plant gets exactly what it needs, when it needs it, it can grow faster and cleaner, in a small space, indoors or outdoors, in any season.

Benefits of hydroponics

  • Uses much less water than soil farming, because the water is recycled
  • Needs less land, so it works on rooftops, balconies and small plots
  • Grows crops all year, regardless of weather or soil quality
  • Reduces pests and soil diseases, so fewer pesticides are needed
  • Often gives faster growth and higher yields

Why hydroponics matters

Right to consume nutritious food. Everyone deserves fresh, safe and nutritious food. Hydroponics lets families and communities grow vegetables and herbs close to home, even in cities with no farmland, so healthy food is within reach instead of being trucked in from far away.

Protecting water and land. Farming draws heavily on both. A hydroponic system recycles its water and grows more food on far less ground, which eases pressure on rivers, groundwater and soil for the generations that follow.

Cleaner, more reliable harvests. With no soil, there are fewer soil-borne pests and diseases, and growing conditions can be controlled. That means less chemical use and a steadier supply of produce through every season.

Hydroponics trends in India

Hydroponics in India has moved from a hobby to a growing industry. Market estimates differ, but most forecast double-digit growth every year for the rest of this decade.

Demand for clean, local produce

Shoppers in cities want pesticide-free vegetables and herbs that are grown nearby and stay fresh. Leafy greens and herbs sold through retail and home delivery are the most common crops.

Farming in small spaces

Rooftops, balconies, warehouses and vertical towers let people grow food where there is little land. Home kits and urban farms are spreading in large cities.

Smart monitoring and automation

Sensors and apps now track nutrient levels, water and temperature, which helps growers get steady results with less guesswork.

Institutions and public sector

Research bodies and public companies are testing hydroponics too. In March 2026, NLC India Limited began growing crops hydroponically in polyhouses on reclaimed mine land, together with the Indian Institute of Science, Bengaluru.

Local equipment and training

Growers still import many parts, such as grow lights and pumps, but more equipment is being made in India. A shortage of trained people and high set-up costs remain the main hurdles.

Frequently asked questions

Can any plant be grown hydroponically?

Many can. Leafy greens, herbs, tomatoes, cucumbers, peppers and strawberries do especially well. Large root crops and trees are harder to grow this way.

Does hydroponics really use less water?

Usually, yes. The water and nutrients are recycled through the system instead of draining into the ground, so far less is wasted.

How much space do I need to start?

Very little. A balcony or a small corner of a terrace is enough for a starter setup, and you can expand later.
